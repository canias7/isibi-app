## A12-6: expired and failed checkouts stayed `pending`

- **Still true.** The Payments panel named only `checkout.session.completed`,
  `failedFromEvent` was never imported, and `async_payment_failed` was handled
  nowhere.
- **Fix.** `failedFromEvent` reads `checkout.session.expired` as `expired` and
  `checkout.session.async_payment_failed` as `failed` (`status`). The site
  webhook moves only a still-pending order (`WHERE … payment_status='pending'`),
  so a late or repeated event never unmarks a paid one. The panel lists all four
  events.
- **Tests.** `test/site-payments-lapsed.test.mjs` (4). Red: 3 of 4 fail on the
  old files. Sweep: 5 of 5 killed, control survived.

## A12-8: the message contract promised local numbers and held messages

- **Still true**, and `test/job-sms-contract.test.mjs` hid it: its phone parser
  was a stub that read local numbers as UK ones, not the runner's `toE164`.
- **Fix.** `MESSAGE_CONTRACT` says a number must be in full international form
  (`+44 7700 900000`), that a local one is dropped, and that a message with no
  key is skipped, not held or sent later. The test now uses the real `toE164`,
  and a new case drives the contract's own quoted examples through it.
- **Red:** the new case fails on the old contract. **Sweep:** 4 of 4 killed,
  control survived.

## A12-9: owner notifications need the owner's key

- **Still true**: the tool text and `PART_SAYS.notify` called the email
  automatic, and a missing key was found only after the five-minute window was
  taken.
- **Fix.** The two tool sentences and `PART_SAYS.notify` say it needs the
  owner's email key and EMAIL_FROM. `notifyOwner` reads the sender (`ready`)
  after winning the claim, and a missing key hands the window back
  (`unclaimNotify` clears only that claim's own `notified_at` stamp). A first
  version read the key before the claim; the review moved it after, because
  that read every submission's vault even on sites with notifications off, and
  the shared vault reader (`siteMailSecrets`) must stay the one reader.
- **Tests.** `test/site-notify-ready.test.mjs` (5). Red: 4 of 5 fail on the old
  files. Sweep: 6 of 6 killed, control survived.
- **Left for the owner: the missed-submission counter.** The claim never returns
  `missed`, so "…and N more since the last email" never appears. Counting needs a
  new column on `site_backends` (a Supabase change). Options: (a) add a
  `notify_missed` integer, incremented by a lost claim and reset by a won one
  (recommended); (b) count submissions since `notified_at` from the site's own
  table at send time (no migration, one extra read per email); (c) leave it and
  remove the line from the email. The doc comment now says the Worker does not
  count them.

## A13-1: a reused request key gets free model calls (owner decision)

Still true (`worker.js`: the ledger ref is `route:<slug>:<key>`, and only
accepted edit/add-on requests are answered without a model). Not changed: it is
a billing change, and the ref's spelling is read exactly by
`scripts/canary-ui.mjs` and four test files. Options:
1. **Store every routed answer under its key** and answer a repeat from it, with
   no model call and no charge (recommended: it also makes retries cheap).
2. Put a digest of the message into the ref (`route:<slug>:<key>:<digest>`), so
   a different message under one key is charged; the canary's ledger reader
   would change with it.
3. Only report `cost: 0` on a repeat (stops the misleading number; the free
   calls stay).

## A13-2: queued edits spend model calls before any balance check (owner decision)

Still true: `/api/site/edit` and `/api/site/addon` file a job with no balance
read, and a refused reserve does not stop later rungs (the loop deliberately
has no `break`). Options:
1. **Refuse at filing below a floor** (`readCreditsFor`, for example 1 credit or
   a per-lane minimum), at no cost (recommended; the floor is a pricing choice).
2. Stop the rung loop at the first `insufficient` reserve, as the data rung's
   `before` hook does (changes the "a failed rung does not cancel the others"
   rule).
3. Both.

## A13-3: a held add-on row in review was refunded from a key it never wrote

- **Still true.** Under DRAFT_FIRST the held path seals a draft revision and
  writes no `_meta` key, so a review closed the empty key and refunded while the
  draft kept the entry for the owner's Publish.
- **Fix.** `reconcileEditJob`: under draft-first, a row job is first read
  against the draft. A revision naming the job is settled by the draft rule
  (`draft-saved`, kept); a draft that cannot be read is unknown and left in
  review (the key is not closed); with no revision of its own, the key decides
  as before.
- **Tests** (`test/addon-row.test.mjs`): the real add-on route under DRAFT_FIRST
  with a refused `edit_committed` (kept, not refunded, the key not closed, the
  live list untouched), and a direct reconcile for the unreadable draft and for
  another job's revision. The test bucket's `put` now answers an object, as R2
  does (the conditional draft save reads it). Red: the route case fails on the
  old `worker.js` (refunded `closed-now`). Sweep: 5 of 5 killed (one survivor
  first, closed by the other-job case), control survived.

## A30-4 and A30-5: idempotency

- **Still true.** The answer was stored only after the upstream answered, so a
  second press in flight wrote too; the spam check ran before the replay lookup,
  so a double-click's reused token was refused as a bot; and the stored id had
  no trace of the body.
- **Fix** (`site-idem.mjs`): a miss claims the key in the isolate until `write`
  or `release`, and a second press of the same contents waits for it and is
  replayed; a refused or failed first press releases it. The stored answer
  carries `fp`, a digest of the body with the spam token taken out
  (`idemFingerprint`), and only the same contents are replayed; other contents
  are a new submission. The data proxy reads the key before the spam gate, and
  releases in a `finally`, on a refusal and on a gate that throws. Checkout reads
  the body once, as text, and ties the key to the basket.
- **Tests.** `test/site-idem-claim.test.mjs` (5: double-click, refused first
  press, thrown first press, changed contents across isolates, a fresh token);
  `test/site-idem.test.mjs`'s order guards updated (read before the gate, the
  digest, the releases); `test/site-payments.test.mjs`'s limit-before-body guard
  follows the body read (`request.text()`). Red: the new file fails on the old
  module. Sweep: 6 of 6 killed after removing a redundant release that a mutant
  showed was dead code; control survived.

## A30-7: half-configured Turnstile refused every booking

- **Fix (Worker).** A TURNSTILE_SECRET without TURNSTILE_SITE_KEY reads as off

- **Tests.** `test/turnstile-half-pair.test.mjs` (source order guards). Red: 2

  key and retry once on a `turnstile` refusal), a template change of its own.

## A30-8: values sent to outside services were URL-encoded everywhere

- **Tests.** `test/site-apis-escape.test.mjs` (3). Red: 2 of 3 fail (the

## A30-9: the overlap rule was dropped before it was re-added

- **Fix.** The new exclusion constraint is added under its own name first
  (`ex_<table>_nooverlap_new`, a leftover of an interrupted apply cleared
  first); only then is the old dropped and the new renamed into place. A refused
  new rule leaves the old standing and is reported; a failed swap leaves the new
  rule standing under its working name and is reported.
- **Tests.** `test/nooverlap-replace.test.mjs` (3, a stand-in database at the
  network). Red: 3 of 3. Sweep: 4 of 4 killed, control survived.
- **Limit.** If a swap failed once, the next apply clears the working-name
  constraint before adding it again: a brief gap in that rare case.
