# 2026-10-23 · decide-sec-3: a wider read is a question back (audit A29-9, #182)

paid, no model calls, no live writes. Not merged or deployed.

## The owner's choice (a)

When a rules change widens who can READ a table from `none` (owner only) or
`own` (each visitor's own rows) to `public` or `members`, the customer is asked
first, unless their own words plainly ask for it. Narrowing, and changes that
do not widen the read, are unchanged.

## What changed

- `builder/site-rules.mjs`
  - The rules tool gives each table a `readWords` field beside `read`: the
    customer's words, copied exactly, that say who should see it.
  - `readRules` carries `readWords` only beside a `read`. `mergeRules` copies
    named rules only, so the quote is never stored.
  - `ungroundedWidenings(changes, tables, asked)` compares before and after
    with `resolveAccess` (never a preset's name). A widening from
    `none`/`own` to `public`/`members` counts as asked for only when its quote
    is really in the request or an answer they gave. The comparison is the one
    `groundRequirements` uses (the same `plain` normaliser, which is now
    exported from `site-requirements.mjs`). No word is looked for.
  - `runRulesEdit` takes `answers`. When any widening has no grounded quote,
    it returns `reason: "ask"` with a question built in code. This happens
    before `before` (billing) and `apply`, so nothing beside the question is
    applied. The cost is the routing call and the one rules call, the same as
    any question the rules step asks today.
- `worker.js`: the rules route passes the answers the step is shown
  (`readContext(eCtx)`), so a confirmed widening applies on the resumed run.
  The existing `stepAsk("rules", …)` keeps and shows the question.

## The question the customer sees

- Public: **"That would let anyone see every entry in bookings. Should I do
  that?"** with the options *Yes, anyone can see them* and *No, keep them
  private*.
- Members: **"That would let anyone signed in to your site see every entry in
  bookings. Should I do that?"** with the options *Yes, signed-in members can
  see them* and *No, keep them private*.

(`bookings` is replaced by the table's name. When several tables would widen,
their names are joined with commas.)

## Tests

- `test/rules-widening.test.mjs`: 12 cases. A widening with no quote, or with
  a quote not in what they wrote, asks and neither bills nor applies, and
  neither does anything proposed beside it. A members widening names signed-in
  members. A widening quoted from the request applies, and so does one quoted
  from an answer. A narrowing applies with no quote, and so does a read that

## Limits (kept honest)

- The check proves the quote is the customer's own words. It does not prove
  those words mean "public". A model could quote an unrelated phrase from the
  request ("see which slots are free") as its basis. The tool tells it to quote
  what says who should see it, and that sentence is all that stands there.
- Widening from `members` to `public` is not asked about, as the choice
  specified.
- The reply after an applied change still says "changed who can see it",
  without the new level. That wording is kept for the model-written replies
  work.
