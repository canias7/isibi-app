## 2. What changed (the connections, on the existing contracts)

- **The frame shows the draft** (`sitePreviewSrc`, `siteDraftFrame`). Under
  draft-first, while the draft is ahead of live, the frame loads the draft's
  own preview host; once nothing is unpublished it is the live address again.
  Only the platform's own draft host is accepted (a foreign or coerced value
  shows the live site). The frame bar says "Draft preview · not published",
  and "updating…" while the preview is not ready.
- **The frame follows the server's readings** (`siteDraftFollow`): a newer
  draft, its preview turning ready, or a publication leaving nothing
  unpublished moves it once. A finished job reads the draft at once
  (`sitePreviewMove`), and Publish moves the frame from its own answer. The
  first version stopped after 20 reads 15 s apart, and at the first failed
  read; §3b replaces that.
- **`GET /draft` says whether the preview is ready** (`previewState` and
  `previewReady`), the one addition to the backend contract. Its first
  version read an empty claim list as ready; that was wrong, and §3b
  replaces it with positive evidence.
- **The request card says what the server records**, as labels only: the
  part a held part waits for ("Waiting for part 2"), a part not run because
  one it needed did not finish, a lost rewrite being recovered, and a part
  saved as a draft ("Saved as a draft", "· preview updating"). Each part
  keeps its own words or the model's line. The view now carries `needs`
  (while held or not run) and `draft` (once done or partly done), from the
  job's own answer or the recovery's reading of the draft.
- **Cancel in the Files panel**: Add and Replace show "Cancel upload" while
  a file goes up; pressed, the upload stops between parts and its session is
  aborted ("Upload cancelled — nothing was kept.").
- **Regular chat is unchanged**, and nothing hardcoded is said as the
  assistant: progress and replies remain the model's own lines from the
  server, and every new string above is a status label.

## 3. The gap found during the batch: a 60-revision cap on the draft

**The owner's requirement**: no change-count limit may force publication or
lose pending work; the draft keeps changing across sessions, and only
Publish releases it.

**Codex's finding.** `site-draft.mjs` cut the revision list to its last 60
(`MAX_REVS`) on every read and every save.
- 100 held row additions were accepted, but `unpublishedOps` and the
  publication plan held only additions 41–100.
- One held addition followed by 99 code saves dropped the addition from the
  draft's effective state.
- Nothing published by itself, but pending work was silently left out.

**The correction** (not a larger cap):
- **Every revision past `applied` is kept**: all unpublished work, and so
  any revision a publication is releasing (it stays past `applied` until
  `markApplied`).
- **Only published revisions are trimmed**, to the latest 20
  (`KEEP_PUBLISHED`), for the panel's history.
- **The trimming is recorded** (`trimmed: { n, at }`). Recovery's
  absence-proof reads it: a lost job that began more than 10 minutes after
  the trimmed revisions were saved left nothing trimmed, so its absence
  decides. A job that began earlier, or at an unknown time, is cannot-tell
  and waits, never refunded on that absence (`revisionsTrimmed`, handed to
  both readers as `draft.trimmed`; the Edit/Add-on rows now carry
  `created_at`). A record cut by the old limit carries no `trimmed` and
  reads as trimmed.
- **Draft versions are kept from the version cap** (`draftKeeps`), even if
  `DRAFT_FIRST` is switched off with a draft pending. A draft that cannot be
  read prunes nothing.

**What remained bounded after this first round** (closed in §3b): every read
of the unpublished changes read one journal per held revision, so the
Worker's per-request subrequest limit would have become the new ceiling.

**Still bounded, and not a limit on the draft:**
- The data step shows the model a table's first `MAX_DATA_ROWS` (60) rows,
  live or held alike. That is a prompt bound, kept separate in the backlog.
- The draft record grows by about 130 bytes per unpublished revision, and
  the cumulative record (§3b) by about 170 bytes per held row change
  (measured at 1,000 revisions: 129,099 and 168,813 bytes).

## 3b. Codex's review of `44676fcd`: two preview defects and the read growth

Codex passed 94 focused tests and confirmed that both retention
reproductions keep every unpublished change; that correction stands.

**(1) An empty claim list was read as ready.**
- **Codex's reproduction:** refuse the preview claim's write, then save.
  `saveAsDraft` says `previewed.ok: false` and uploads nothing, but `GET
  /draft` said `previewReady: true` because no claim stood.
- **The fix:**
  - **Positive evidence.** A writer that confirms the preview is serving
    the draft's selected version (`convergePreview` ready) now records that
    version (`preview-up/<slug>.json`, `markPreviewUp`) before it drops its
    claim. The record only moves forward: versions sort by minting time and
    the draft's version never goes back, so a late, older confirmation
    never replaces a newer one. A record that cannot be written keeps the
    claim; nothing is called ready without it.
  - **Readiness** (`previewReadiness`): `ready` only when the record names
    the draft's selected version and no claim stands; `pending` otherwise;
    `failed` when the tick has given up (its register is stuck); `unknown`
    when the record or the claims cannot be read.
  - **Repair.** A save whose claim was refused leaves no claim, so no
    writer would finish it. When the owner's read finds nothing confirmed
    for this version and no upload standing, it starts one repair after its
    answer, under its own claim and within the register's backoff
    (`readPreviewState`, `repairPreview`, the tick's own step, factored
    out). The tick does the same for claims that can no longer land, and
    records before dropping.

**(2) The page stopped following too soon.**
- **Codex's reproduction:** the server turned ready at minute 16; the page
  had stopped after 20 reads 15 s apart (5 minutes), or at the first 503,
  and stayed "updating" with no timer.
- **The fix** (`siteDraftFollowAgain`, `siteDraftUnsettled`): an unsettled
  draft is read again on a backoff for as long as the site's workspace is
  open and the tab visible. "Unsettled" means a preview pending, failed or
  unknown, work still being saved or published, or a failed read. The
  backoff runs 5 s, doubling, capped at 2 minutes, with no count limit.
  - A change in what the frame depends on starts the backoff over.
  - Switching sites, closing the workspace, hiding the tab or leaving the
    page stops it; coming back reads again.
  - The bar says "updating…", "checking…" or "couldn't update the preview
    yet" from the server's state.

**(3) Reads grew with the draft.**
- **Measured before** (`readDraft`, then `unpublishedOps` twice, then one
  more held save and a third read; an in-memory store counting calls):
  - 10, 100, 500 and 1,000 storage reads for as many held revisions;
  - the same on every read, and N + 1 after one more save.
- **The fix: one cumulative record** (`site-drafts/<slug>/held-sum.json`)
  of the unpublished held ops, each revision's entry `{ n, held, count }`.
  - **Never trusted beyond what it proves.** An entry is used only when
    both its revision number and its journal id match, since journals are
    immutable and named by a random id. Counts that do not add up void the
    whole record.
  - **Folding.** A revision without a matching entry is read from its
    journal and folded in. Each held save folds its own revision at once;
    a publication drops published entries rather than voiding the record.
  - **Unchanged:** the journals, exact revision ranges, and recovery's
    reads of the journals.
- **Measured after:**
  - **reads:** 1 storage read at 10, 100, 500 and 1,000 revisions, after
    one more save too;
  - **each save:** 3 reads and 3 writes at every size (the draft record,
    the journal, the cumulative record).

## 3c. Codex's review of `668ebc87`: the container's gateway and a cache that never repaired

CI for `668ebc87` passed; Codex then reproduced two gaps independently.

**(1) A container job's own draft records were refused at the gateway.**
- **Codex's reproduction:** through the real `GatewayBucket` →
  `gatewayHandler` connection, the job's own records all answered 403:
  `site-drafts/<slug>.json`, `site-drafts/<slug>/held/<id>.json`,
  `site-drafts/<slug>/held-sum.json` and `preview-up/<slug>.json`.
  - `saveAsDraft` answered "draft: gateway get 403".
  - `hold.flush` failed.
  - `markPreviewUp` answered false.
- **Why no test saw it:** `test/fixtures/fired-jobs.mjs` handed fired jobs
  the platform's bucket directly.
- **The fix** (`builder/job-gateway.mjs` `allowedJobKey`) admits exactly the
  job's own site's draft record, its held journals (named by a 24-hex id),
  the cumulative record and the preview's confirmation. It never admits
  another site's records (a name that merely starts the same included), a
  pre-scoped job's, the publication's journal (`site-publishing/`, the
  owner's Publish only) or a malformed id.
- **Two more refusals, found once the fired jobs ran through the real
  gateway, and neither draft-specific:**
  - **Script notes:** a live publication's note
    (`scriptpending/<slug>/<uuid>.json`, written around the site script's
    upload). Refused, a container job's publish stopped before uploading.
    **`main` (deploy 2214) has this gap**: its gateway refuses the note its
    own publish writes.
  - **Build markers:** a build's own marker in its owner's listing
    (`builds-live/<uid>/<id>.json` and `.done.json`), admitted only for this
    job's id under its token's own uid (`ownBuildMarker`). Refused, a
    container build could not keep its final answer for a later read (a
    best-effort step).
- **Not a gap, so not admitted.** Two other refusals (reading and writing
  the request's record) came from the fixture's env lacking the
  container's own `JOB_NEXT` hook, which asks the Worker to move the
  request on. With the real `makeContainerEnv` they disappear, so nothing
  was widened for them.

**(2) A malformed cumulative record never repaired.**
- **Codex's reproduction:** `readHeldSum` dropped the object's etag when its
  body would not parse. The rewrite was then create-only against an object
  that existed, so it was refused every time: 100 journal reads on each of
  two requests, with the cache unchanged.
- **The fix:** the object's etag is kept whatever its body says, and an
  unusable record is replaced conditionally on it, even when nothing new
  was learned. A read that failed outright still writes nothing over what
  it could not see.

**The fixtures, now the real connection:**
- `test/fixtures/job-gateway-wire.mjs`: the real shim and handler, either
  over a given bucket (with a token signed by `signJobToken` and the scope
  op wired) or through the Worker's own route (`worker.fetch` →
  `jobGateway`).
- `test/fixtures/fired-jobs.mjs`: with `worker`, it builds the job's env

- **Two stand-in corrections they exposed:**
  - the request-flow bucket's objects now carry R2's `body` stream (the

  - the compiler stand-in answers the build service's `/model` door when a
