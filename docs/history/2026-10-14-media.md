# Video and audio, upload to playback (2026-10-14, branch `new`)

The owner's request (after Codex's review of `9ceca1d4`): *"implement complete
owner video/audio upload-to-playback infrastructure. Component creation is not
the focus … Extend the existing upload, storage, serving and file-management
system so owners can upload supported media, obtain lasting site-scoped URLs,
and use those assets through Build, Edit and Add-on. Cover attachments before a
site exists as well as uploads to an existing site, with durable storage and
correct ownership transfer when the site is created. Classify media explicitly
… Validate supported formats from file contents, use correct MIME types,
support browser playback and seeking with appropriate Range/HEAD responses, and
use a large-file upload approach that avoids buffering entire videos through
the Worker. Choose and document practical configurable per-file and per-site
limits … Preserve assets through publishing, edits, retries and reconnects. Let
owners list, select, replace and delete media, with defined behavior for
existing references, safe cache handling, correct usage accounting and
isolation between sites. Give the model the actual media URLs and available
metadata, without pretending it watched or listened … Keep existing
image/document behavior working and do not automatically enable visitor media
uploads."*

Unmerged, undeployed. No paid model call, no generated media, no new service:
the bytes go to the R2 bucket the site uploads already use (`SITES_BUCKET`).
The test fixtures are nine small local files made with ffmpeg on this machine
(two-second sine tones and a four-second 64×48 test pattern, 116 KB in all).

## 1. What was there

- `site-uploads.mjs` sniffed pictures (PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF) and documents (PDF,
  the zip family) and refused everything else (415).
- A stored file was classified as "not a document", so every non-document read
  as a picture: `uploadIsImage("interview.mp3")` was true (the comment said so).
- The owner route read the whole body (`request.arrayBuffer()`), 10 MB at most;
  per site 200 files and 100 MB.
- `/u/<slug>/<file>` served `obj.body` whole: no `Range`, no `206`, no `HEAD`
  handling, no `ETag`, `immutable` for a year.
- The composer turned a video into one still frame (an image for the model) and
  refused audio; nothing kept a video before a site existed.
- The picture lane's library, the link-preview fallback and the photo headroom
  counted every upload as a picture.

## 2. The design (`site-media.mjs`, the module's own header says it in full)

**Formats, from the contents.** Video: MP4 (and M4V; ISO brands isom/iso2–9,
mp41/42, avc1, dash, M4V…), WebM (EBML with doc type `webm`), QuickTime MOV
(`ftyp qt  `, or a leading `moov`/`mdat`/`wide` atom). Audio: MP3 (ID3, or an
MPEG frame sync), M4A (`ftyp M4A `/`M4B `), AAC (ADTS), WAV (`RIFF…WAVE`), Ogg
(Vorbis or Opus), FLAC. Anything else — a 3GP brand, Matroska that is not WebM,
a PNG called `.mp4` — is refused. The declared name and type are never
believed: the first part's bytes decide, must agree with the declared kind and
format, and the stored type is the one the bytes named (`video/mp4`,
`video/webm`, `video/quicktime`, `audio/mpeg`, `audio/mp4`, `audio/aac`,
`audio/wav`, `audio/ogg`, `audio/flac`).

**Classification, explicit.** `uploadKind(name)` answers image, doc, video,
audio, or null; `uploadIsImage` is now `uploadKind(name) === "image"`. The
listing, the panel, the picture lane's library, the link-preview fallback, the
photo headroom and the conversation's context all read it. `IMAGE_EXTS` is
still exactly the four picture formats.

**Upload, in parts — never a whole video through the Worker.**
1. `POST /api/site/<slug>/media` `{ name, size, type, replace?, meta? }` — the
   declared kind's per-file limit and the site's limits are checked, counting
   uploads in flight; an R2 multipart upload is created at the file's final key
   with its type and metadata; a session record is stored
   (`media-sessions/site-<slug>/<id>.json`, 24 h). Answers the session, the
   part size (8 MiB) and the number of parts.
2. `PUT …/media/<session>/<n>` — one part, refused above 8 MiB on its declared
   length before anything is read, and refused unless its length is exactly what
   part n must be. Part 1's bytes are sniffed (a refused first part aborts the
   upload). The part goes to R2 (`uploadPart`).
3. `POST …/media/<session>/complete` `{ parts: [{ n, etag }] }` — every part,
   1…N, once; R2 joins them and the file appears at its address only now.
4. `DELETE …/media/<session>` — gives the upload up (`abort`).

A part that fails is sent again alone; a session lives on a record, so a part
can reach any Worker after any reconnect; an abandoned upload never appears,
reserves its size only until its session expires, and is aborted when next met
(R2 itself also aborts unfinished multipart uploads after seven days). Nothing
holds more than one part (8 MiB), inside every plan's request-size limit.

**Names and addresses.** 32 random hex characters plus the extension the bytes
decided, under `uploads/<slug>/` (outside `sites/<slug>/`, which a publish
replaces), served at `/u/<slug>/<name>` on the app's origin and at the root of
the site's own address, like every upload. Not content-addressed (a video is
never hashed whole), so a replacement can keep the address.

**Limits** (`mediaLimits(env)`), separate from the pictures' and documents'
(which are unchanged: 5 MB / 10 MB a file, 200 files and 100 MB a site):

| | default | environment variable | ceiling |
|---|---|---|---|
| a video file | 250 MB | `MEDIA_MAX_VIDEO_BYTES` | 2 GB |
| an audio file | 50 MB | `MEDIA_MAX_AUDIO_BYTES` | 500 MB |
| video and audio per site | 2 GB | `MEDIA_MAX_SITE_BYTES` | 20 GB |
| files per site | 100 | `MEDIA_MAX_SITE_FILES` | 1,000 |
| files kept before a site exists | 6 | `MEDIA_MAX_DRAFT_FILES` | 20 |

A value that does not read as a positive whole number is the default; one above
its ceiling is the ceiling. Nothing is unlimited. None of these variables is set
in `wrangler.jsonc`, so the defaults apply until the owner sets one.

**Serving** (`/u/` in `worker.js`). `GET` and `HEAD` only (405 otherwise). The
object's head is read first; then: `If-None-Match` naming its ETag → `304`; a
`Range` of one range → `206` with `Content-Range` and only those bytes read
from R2; past the end → `416` with `bytes */size`; several ranges → the whole
file (200); `HEAD` → the headers with no body. Always `Accept-Ranges: bytes`,
`Content-Length`, `ETag`, `nosniff`, `inline`. A video or recording is cached
`public, max-age=300, must-revalidate` (it can be replaced at its address); a
picture or document keeps `public, max-age=31536000, immutable` (its name is its
content).

**Listing, replacing, deleting.**
- `GET /api/site/<slug>/uploads` lists every file with its kind; a video or
  recording also with its type, its display name and what the owner's browser
  reported (length, frame size). The pictures' and documents' allowance
  (`used`, `max`, `count`, `maxCount`) counts only them; `media` carries the
  video and audio allowance, the per-file limits and the formats.
- Replace: the same start with `replace: <name>` — same kind and same format
  only; it writes over the same key when the last part is in, so every page
  that plays it plays the new file; its ETag changes, so a cached copy
  revalidates within five minutes. Counted as the difference in size.
- Delete: a video or recording whose address a stored page names is not deleted
  — `409 in_use` with the pages — until the owner confirms (`?force=1`). The
  page then still names the address and its player stops working; the panel
  says so before. Pictures and documents delete as before.

**Before a site exists.** The same upload at `/api/media/drafts`, for the
signed-in account only: kept at `drafts/<uid>/<name>` (never served), at most
`draftFiles`, for seven days (expired ones removed as they are met); listed and
removable by their owner only. A first build's message names them (`media`);
once the build has claimed the address, `adoptDraftMedia` moves each — checked
to be this account's draft, within the site's limits — into
`uploads/<slug>/<same name>`, streamed (never held whole), with its type and
metadata, and then removes the draft. Another account's draft is never found
(the lookup is under the caller's own uid). What could not be moved is said in
the build's reply (`media.missing`, and a sentence in `contextNote`).

**The models.** Every page writer — a build's, an edit's page step, an add-on's —
gets the site's video and audio through `briefWithLayout` (`mediaBlock`): each
file's display name, address, format, kind, size and the browser-reported
length and frame size, with how to play it (`<video>`/`<audio>`, `controls`,
`playsInline`, `preload="metadata"`) and *"Nobody has watched or listened to
these files … never say what is in one."* The build's designer is told the
names and kinds of the drafts moving in (`mediaDesignNote`). The conversation
lists the library with kinds and the browser's report. A video is never offered
as a photograph to reuse, never a link preview, never in the picture lane's
library.

**The page.**
- Files panel: Add a file uploads a video or recording in parts with its
  percentage on the button; a video tile shows its first frame, a recording its
  format and a small player; each has *Use in chat* (puts it in the composer as
  a ready attachment) and *Replace*; the hint and the bar show both allowances
  from the server. Deleting a file a page plays asks again, naming the pages.
- Composer: a video or recording attached goes up at once — to the site's
  library, or kept for the account before there is a site — as a chip that says
  *uploading n%*, *ready* or *not uploaded*; it takes none of the three places
  for pictures and documents; a message waits (said) while one is still
  sending; sent, the message names each file (`[Attached video "Tour.mp4":
  /u/<slug>/<name>]`) and a first build also sends the drafts' names (`media`).
  Taking a chip out while it sends cancels it and gives the parts up.
- `public/site-media.js` (`SiteMedia`) holds the upload's decisions, testable.

**Kept as it was.** Pictures and documents: same sniffers, same limits, same
names, same immutable serving. Visitor uploads: pictures only, unchanged — no
visitor video or audio. The legacy `/a/` route is untouched.

## 4. What is not shown

- A real R2 multipart upload, part etags and completion; the streamed draft
  move (`FixedLengthStream`); R2's default abort of unfinished uploads — all
  shown only against a stand-in that checks parts as R2 does.
- MP4, MOV, M4A and AAC playback in a real browser (this Chromium has no
  H.264/AAC decoder); HEVC in a MOV, and Firefox with MOV, may not play.
- The live edge with range responses and the five-minute cache; throughput;
  a custom domain's `/u/` (the same handler, `servedAtRoot`).
- Drafts are cleaned when their account's drafts are next listed or used, not
  on a schedule; there is no R2 lifecycle rule.
- The composer's chips in a real browser (pure tests and source guards only).
- Not built, by design: visitor video or audio, transcoding, posters, captions,
  any reading of a file's contents.
