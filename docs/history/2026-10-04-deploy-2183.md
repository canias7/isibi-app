### 7.5 Why the reply check failed: the canary judged it while it was still being written

**What the page was handed.** The page read the menu job's answer twice, at
about 23:34:27.7 and 23:34:28.9 UTC (its network log, 203.1 s and 204.4 s),
and both times it was the outcome with **`replyState: "pending"`,
`replyOutcome: "done"`**: the job had ended at 23:34:21.2 and its reply was
being written in the background. The page applied the outcome and held the
reply's place with *"Done — writing up what changed…"* (the screenshot at the
step's end, `ui-step-1.png`, shows that line as the thread's last).

**Where the canary stops.** In request mode a message ends when the composer is
ready, the request has ended and the page has closed it
(`scripts/canary-ui.mjs`, `followStep`). Since the owner's review round
(`2026-10-04-review-round.md` §3) a part's reply still being written no longer
keeps the page's request open: its outcome is applied, its place held, and
the reply followed on its own. So the page closed the request at once, the
canary ended the message 8 seconds after the job ended, and it judged the
reply from the last answer the page had been handed: pending, so not the
model's. **The canary was not taught the held reply when the page was**, and
nothing in its tests covered it. This is a gap in the instrument from that
round, not a reading of the reply writer.

**Whether the reply was then written is not established.** The canary closed
the page before the page's follow could settle the line. The reply's record is
in R2, which this session cannot read, and the owner's poll needs the owner's
session. Supabase's request logs show:
- no read of the job (`rpc/edit_get`, which the writer makes when it takes a
  reply) after the canary's own at 23:34:37.9, up to 23:42:49 (the last row
  available then); only the two-minute sweeps (`edit_sweep_lost`,
  `edit_sweep_stale`, two reads of `edit_jobs`) at 23:34:40, 23:36:40,
  23:38:41, 23:40:52 and 23:42:49;
- under the reply's own timing (`REPLY_BG_RETRY_S` 30 s, `REPLY_RETRY_GRACE_MS`
  90 s, the sweep every two minutes) a retry, a claim run out or a lost ask
  would have read the job again by 23:36:40;
- so the record most likely settled on its first try, between 23:34:21 and
  23:34:29: written, owed nothing, or failed for good. Which is not shown.

**A free press would show it**: the canary's read-one-job mode on
`f666481af2ef5410b14e00b9ad0da43d` polls the job as the owner and keeps the
whole answer in its evidence file (`job-<id>.json`, `poll.body`): `reply` with
`replySource: "model"` if it was written, `replyState: "failed"` if not.

  now run Saturday bread-making classes, and I added a Classes page that

**Why.** The canary pressed Send about 2 seconds after the site opened. The
page's check of the site's requests (`siteRequestsCheck`) answered after that.
It follows every request the server lists that this browser has not closed (a
fresh browser has closed none), and appends each part's reply, and the
request's own, at the end of the thread. They landed under the message just
sent, where they read as its replies. **R1's request reply was written then**:
the page read R1's request as pending for about 35 seconds (seven reads, 3.8 s
to 38.1 s) before it was handed that sentence. A request's reply with no
record is asked for whenever it is read, however old; a job's is not asked
for more than two hours after it ended (`REPLY_ASK_WINDOW_MS`). R1 had ended
about 20 hours earlier.

**What it shows, live**: a request's own reply written in the background and
served (one sample, about 35 s). It changed nothing on the site and cost the
customer nothing. Both halves are recorded in the backlog; neither is fixed.
